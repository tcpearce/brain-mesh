# P001 claims: heating in water lowers binding capacity because the film swells

**Main claim.** Heating the imprinted film in [[water]] lowers its [[binding-capacity|binding capacity]] because [[swelling]] of the polymer network distorts the cavities ([[P001-007-swelling-distorts-cavities|P001-007]]).

**Sub-claims**
1. Binding capacity falls from 4.2 at 25 °C to 1.3 at 45 °C in water ([[P001-003-capacity-4-2-at-25c-water|P001-003]], [[P001-004-capacity-1-3-at-45c-water|P001-004]]).
2. The film thickens by 18% over the same range, which is the swelling the main claim invokes ([[P001-005-film-thickens-18-percent|P001-005]]).

**How far the evidence carries it.** The fall in capacity is measured, three films per temperature. That swelling causes it is the authors' inference: thickness and capacity change together, but nothing separates swelling from heat itself, and organic solvents were not tested ([[P001-008-organic-solvents-not-tested|P001-008]]).
