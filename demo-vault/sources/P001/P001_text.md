Temperature dependence of analyte X binding by an imprinted polymer film A. Smith and B. Jones. Journal of Test Materials 12, 100-108 (2021). doi:10.0000/jtm.2021.001 ^b0001

Abstract We measured the binding capacity of an imprinted polymer film for analyte X between 25 and 45 °C in water. Binding capacity decreased from 4.2 to 1.3 as temperature rose. We attribute the loss to swelling of the film. ^b0002

Methods Films were prepared by spin coating at 2000 rpm and cured for 12 h. Binding capacity was determined by a batch rebinding assay with UV detection at 254 nm, using three replicate films per condition. ^b0003

Results At 25 °C the binding capacity was 4.2 ± 0.3 (n = 3). At 45 °C it decreased to 1.3 ± 0.2 (n = 3). Film thickness increased by 18% between 25 and 45 °C. Unexpectedly, one film at 35 °C showed a transient increase in capacity during the first 5 minutes, which we could not explain. ^b0004

## Page 2

Discussion We propose that swelling of the polymer network distorts the imprinted cavities and so lowers binding capacity. We did not test organic solvents. The assay assumes that binding reaches equilibrium within 60 minutes. Prior work reported that swelling scales with temperature in hydrogels (Lee 2015). Whether the capacity loss is reversible on cooling remains unknown. ^b0005
